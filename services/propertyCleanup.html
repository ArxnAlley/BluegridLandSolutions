  • Common Questions Questions We Get Every Week Straight answers on pricing, stumps, timing, and what actually happens the day the machine shows up. View All FAQs
    • What is forestry mulching? One tracked machine grinds standing brush, briars, and small trees into chips right where they stand.
    • How do you price a job? By the acre or by the day, whichever fits the ground better.
    • Are you licensed and insured? Yes, and we send a certificate of insurance before the machine leaves the shop.
    • Is brush hogging better than mulching? A mulcher takes stems up to eight or ten inches and grinds them flush; a brush hog leaves the woody stems behind.
    • Do you remove stumps? Mulching cuts at the stump and leaves the root mat holding the ground together.
    • How soon can you start? Most quotes go out within 24 hours, and scheduling usually runs a couple of weeks out.

Property Cleanup in Southern Ohio & Eastern Kentucky

Overgrown, vacant, or inherited ground brought back to something you can walk, show, sell, or finally use again.

Property cleanup is the whole-place version of the work, and it usually starts with a life event rather than a landscaping plan. A place gets inherited. A rental sits empty for three years. Someone gets older and the mowing stops at the edge of the yard, and the field quietly moves in.

The work is a mix: brush and volunteer growth mulched down, sight lines reopened, road frontage exposed, pond banks and outbuildings cleared back to where you can reach them, and debris uncovered and piled so it can actually be dealt with.

We are used to working in stages, because most people do not want to spend it all at once. Open the access and the yard first, decide about the back forty later. If the goal is a building site rather than a presentable property, land clearing is the next step; if a storm caused it, start with storm cleanup.

What Changes

What Property Cleanup Covers

Sight Lines Back

Views you forgot the property had: the pond, the ridge, the road frontage. Buyers and appraisers cannot value ground they cannot see across.

Structures Reachable Again

Barns, sheds, wells, and outbuildings cleared back so you can inspect them, repair them, or decide honestly whether they are worth keeping.

Staged to Your Budget

Access and the yard this year, the back acreage next. We price it in phases so you are not forced to do the whole place in one invoice.

Careful Around the Unknown

Neglected ground hides wire, posts, cisterns, and scrap. The operator works deliberately in the rough stuff instead of finding it the hard way.

Debris Found and Piled

We expose what has been swallowed and stack it where a roll-off can reach, so you can size a container instead of guessing at what is out there.

Fast Enough to Matter

One to two acres a day on typical neglect. A property that has been an embarrassment for a decade can look managed by the end of the week.

Property Cleanup Questions

Start with access and sight lines. Getting a lane opened and the area around the house or barn cleared lets you actually assess what you own, which is usually the real obstacle. From there you can decide whether the rest gets cleaned up, listed, or left. We are used to working in stages for exactly this reason.

We clear the vegetation and can expose and pile debris so it is reachable, but hauling off tires, appliances, and scrap is a different service with its own disposal costs and regulations. Most clients have us open the ground up first, then bring in a roll-off. We will tell you what we found so you can size the container correctly.

It usually helps more than anything else you could spend the same money on. Buyers cannot value what they cannot see or walk. Opening sight lines, exposing the road frontage, and making the acreage walkable lets a buyer picture using it, and lets an appraiser measure what is actually there.

Yes. Tracked machines are gentler on turf than most people expect, especially when the ground is firm. We work from the rough side inward, keep the carrier off septic fields and soft spots, and where the ground is genuinely too wet we will recommend waiting rather than leaving you with ruts to repair.

On ground that has been let go for five to ten years, expect one to two acres a day.
